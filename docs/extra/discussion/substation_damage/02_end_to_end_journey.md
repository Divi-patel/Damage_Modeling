# Substation — the growing Damage journey

This is the Damage chapter of the [Hazard end-to-end journey](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Hazard_modeling/blob/509bf435e1990a5d04d59c0c34621cc2d9a82fdb/docs/discussion/_cross_cutting/substation_conus/02_end_to_end_journey.md).
Append decisions and lessons as they occur; keep current implementation truth in the eventual admitted cell.

## 2026-10-16 — step zero and the first Damage pass

1. **Start with the idealistic twin.** The owner's artifact showed a rich 345/138 kV transmission station.
   Hazard preserved its source/node hashes and reduced 38 nodes into one identity, five systems and one
   excluded floodwall. The diagram reveals candidate mechanisms; its sample costs/hazard scores/losses
   are not calibrated evidence.
2. **Choose the intended delivery.** One canonical station repeated across CONUS, initially direct physical
   loss. No observed site, fictitious ownership, generation MW or outage valuation was invented. Real Estate
   profiles/handoff were completed separately; Junga owns their Damage work.
3. **Check the profile and value.** Hazard fixed the selected geometry/equipment quantities and rebuilt an
   approximately $30M installed-cost reference from MISO tables and explicit allowances. A useful budget is
   not automatically a curve denominator. Its exact published pin is the input to this Damage work.
4. **Read existing inventory first.** Flood/Wind already contains a released source-native substation
   response. Its source is a whole assembly. The component inventory remains reusable research anatomy,
   while numerical component curves remain absent. The Wind cell name does not itself prove or disprove
   standalone applicability.
5. **Separate four proofs.** Numerical equality, physical applicability, economic denominator and allowed
   metrics are independent. We reproduced 93 cases and reconciled 31 cost rows. These complete only the
   numerical/accounting checks; no new substation response was admitted.
6. **Expose the material mismatch.** Source control-room damage begins from grade and its switchgear
   assumption is +3 ft. Our floor is +0.3 m; +1 m refers to a transformer control cabinet, not all switchgear.
   Do not shift the entire assembly input or call this an exact-design model.
7. **Make the decision understandable before asking.** The initial owner question about accepting a
   screening proxy was premature. The owner asked what it meant. We explained whole-assembly versus
   component response with the 4-ft/7% example. This is not proxy approval. Complete the physical/value
   comparison before seeking a concrete decision.
8. **Document access honestly.** Fresh FEMA requests returned 403; prior governed source/table evidence
   remains. Freshly inspected NERC transmission-station evidence supports water-entry/elevation mechanisms,
   without supplying repair-dollar curves. No universal claim about missing component evidence is made.
9. **Improve the existing skill.** Revision 0.8 adds a reusable qualification-progress table and inherited
   versus fresh source verification, and corrects the distinction between curve spread and annual hazard
   variability. It does not add a registry or change cell/schema versions.
10. **Keep the next boundary explicit.** Source/value disposition → concrete proxy choice → governed
    standalone candidate → conditional-event proof → admitted annual M0–M4 slice → full CONUS → release
    and readable notebook. No step is completed merely by having a profile or this documentation.

11. **Separate station function from modeling grain.** The owner questioned the inherited GSU/plant context
    and expected distribution or switching. The current candidate is actually transmission transformation
    (345/138 kV, two 336 MVA autotransformers). Historical records show that the assembly response was chosen
    for its source grain and limited component evidence, not merely because the station was a subsystem.
    The [type comparison](04_station_type_and_response_grain.md) distinguishes functional profile, model
    boundary and response resolution. Same source percentages across voltage classes do not prove all
    station types have the same vulnerability. No new type or proxy is selected by this question.

12. **Scope resolved by simplifying:** the owner chose a single generic canonical-substation grid and asked
    us to reuse existing GSU work. Separate station-type products are deferred under SD-D11. Code inspection
    confirms a Solar Flood transformer/switchgear route as well as the separate Wind-hosted assembly. Their
    axes, value scopes and annual capabilities differ; existing evidence audits already identify transfer
    limits. Reuse that work, retain one concrete physical reference, and implement a standalone binding.

13. **Clarify the multi-hazard destination before execution:** the owner asked which other hazards already
    treat substations and how Total will work. The [inventory](05_cross_hazard_reuse_inventory.md) finds
    numeric Flood, Hurricane and Wildfire candidates, explicit withheld units, and older Convective Wind
    station assumptions whose exposure/value semantics were rejected. Current Hail work does not supply
    a station response. Hazard's linked roadmap owns the readiness-based order and Total integration;
    Damage owns each response's qualification. This documents the broader endpoint without starting every
    hazard at once or claiming a standalone grid already exists.

14. **Made the first binding executable:** the [candidate review](06_flood_binding_candidate_and_proof.md)
    preserves the exact existing curve and prices its ratios against a clearly proposed $27.353M fenced-station
    assembly. All 31 rows reconcile; 16 test methods pass. Audit dollars are separate from null reportable
    scenario/whole-station/annual outputs. Primary-PDF retrieval still returned 403, so provenance remains
    inherited rather than freshly verified. Native Riverine input hashes match the selected reference run,
    but their 100 MW Solar placements cannot be renamed as station exposure. Next is a native Pluvial
    station-coupling diagnostic and completion of transfer/value admission. SD-D13 also preserves reuse
    back into Solar/Wind through explicit versions; no parent model changed here.

15. **Hazard returned an executable coupling candidate:** the owner asked us to proceed and confirmed
    boundary readiness. The physical rectangle and inventory already exist. Hazard's selected development
    method uses one geometric-centre facility point with the whole boundary retained for fit/support;
    its executed comparison also exposes dry-point/pondable-yard cases. The
    [handoff](07_hazard_point_coupling_handoff.md) records what Damage must finish: transfer/value
    qualification, a standalone capability and the annual-domain/support proof. The existing curve and
    annual prohibition remain unchanged. No new survey or station-type research is a prerequisite.

16. **Preserved component research and returned to delivery:** the owner considered the standalone
    component workflow, then explicitly prioritized high-level Substation CONUS grids across important
    hazards. The [future plan](../../../plans/substation_damage/component_research_backlog.md) preserves
    the skill steps, reuse candidates, 31-row feasibility audit and datum mismatch for coordination with
    the intern. No component model was released. Active Hazard work returns to the assembly screening
    response and nested 0.05°/0.25° architecture; annual and support gates still apply.
