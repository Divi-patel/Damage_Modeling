# Decision log — hail_real_estate model v0.1 / docs r1

Entries are **proposed** for JL's decision unless marked **JL decided** (2026-10-18). JL's decisions are scientific
choices for this proposal; the cell as a whole still needs owner review before any canonical use. Claims (HRE-C…)
and sources (HRE-S…) are in the [claim register](CLAIM_PARAMETER_REGISTER_hail_real_estate__model_v0_1__docs_r1.csv)
and [source register](SOURCE_REGISTER_hail_real_estate__model_v0_1__docs_r1.csv).

| ID | Decision | Reason | Alternatives considered | Revisit trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D-HRE-01 | **JL decided — roof-cover curves.** Each building's curve is the chance its roof cover needs replacing at a given hail size (Haag material thresholds), and its cost comes from a share of building value (FEMA Hazus cost ratios). The Swiss radar-claims curve (Schmid et al. 2024) is kept only as a cross-check. | No published US building hail curve exists, and Hazus has no hail model (HRE-C019). The roof thresholds are US engineering evidence specific to each roof type. The Swiss curve averages over every building in a 1 km pixel, most of them unhit, and covers Swiss tile roofs: 0.05% at 60 mm (HRE-C014). | Swiss curve for all three buildings (T4 transfer); record hail as a documented gap. | A US radar-to-claims study by roof type. |
| D-HRE-02 | One cell, `hail_real_estate`, for the three Hazard reference profiles r1: house `RE_RES_SFD_1S_SLAB`, warehouse `RE_IND_WAREHOUSE`, office `RE_COM_OFFICE_3S_SLAB`. The reference building is the routing selector. Retail is not carried. | Same building identities as the flood, hurricane and convective real-estate cells, so losses on one building can be combined. | Separate cells per building. | Hazard adds a profile. |
| D-HRE-03 | One pathway with the repository's existing ID, `hail_impact` (the hail × wind proposal uses the same ID). The axis is `mesh_diameter_mm` exactly as Hazard delivers it (the hail × solar field). Damage reads it as the largest hailstone at the building and applies no bridge. | Hazard sends MESH75 for each native pixel and no damage below raw MESH 25.4 mm, so the smallest nonzero input is 38.98 mm (HRE-C013). The roof thresholds are stated in maximum stone size. The MESH75 floor is a Hazard-side item (D-HRE-16). | Convert MESH75 back to raw MESH in Damage; a separate inch field. | Hazard changes the transform or adds a ground-truth bridge. |
| D-HRE-04 | **JL decided — failure units.** `HRE_ROOF_COVER` (primary); `HRE_WALLS_AND_WINDOWS`, a separate Tier-4 screening unit; `HRE_EXPOSED_EQUIPMENT` (AC condenser, rooftop units) withheld, not zero; `HRE_BUILDING_CONTENTS` withheld. | The solar and wind cells split assets into parts. Weak parts either get a labelled Tier-4 curve (tropical-cyclone × solar, wildfire × wind) or are withheld. No cell folds weak parts into a sourced curve. Fin denting is mostly comb-repairable (HRE-C016). | Fold walls and windows into the roof curve; roof only, walls and windows as a gap. | Wall, window or equipment hail evidence. |
| D-HRE-05 | **JL decided — real damage only.** Only damage that impairs function is counted: punctured, fractured or ruptured cover, or open seams. Cosmetic denting of the metal roof, metal walls, overhead doors and gutters is excluded. Counting cosmetic metal-roof denting (from 1.5 in) is recorded as an alternative case in SENSITIVITY and NRI_CHECK. | The y-axis is physical repair cost. Haag defines damage as reduced water-shedding or service life (HRE-C003, C005). The reviewer's mechanism guide asks for cosmetic denting to be kept separate from loss of function. Paying for dents is a policy matter. | Count cosmetic denting (warehouse yearly loss rises from about 0 to about the house's level). | A consumer asks for policy-style loss. |
| D-HRE-06 | **JL decided — y-axis on the part's own value.** Each unit's damage ratio is a fraction of that unit's own replacement cost. The unit's share of structure replacement cost new is in `failure_units[].share_of_structure_rcn` and the value crosswalk. Building level = Σ unit DR × unit share, also printed in SENSITIVITY and the return package. | This matches the solar and wind cells. The repository lists "whole value as a component denominator" as a known failure mode. The building-level figure stays comparable with the other real-estate cells. | Express each unit directly as % of whole-building value. | — |
| D-HRE-07 | Roof thresholds. House: laminated shingles, onset 1.25 in, full 2.0 in. Office: TPO, 1.0–2.0 in. Warehouse: standing-seam steel, onset 2.5 in, full 3.5 in. | Haag field and lab tables (HRE-C001, C002, C004, C005). The test shingles (11 years old) match the profile's 10-year roof age. The profile does not state shingle type, so laminated, the common modern type, is assumed, with 3-tab as the high case. Warehouse full size is judgment (HRE-C021). | 3-tab shingles; adhered TPO on a cover board (1.5–2.0 in); warehouse full at 3.0 or 4.0 in. | Hazard states shingle type, TPO attachment or panel gauge. |
| D-HRE-08 | Curve form. Each part's replacement probability is lognormal in hail diameter, with onset = 5% and full = 95% (median = √(onset·full), β = ln(full/onset)/3.29). A unit's DR = Σ (part value / unit value) × hail-facing share × P. The curve is tabulated every 1 mm from 0 to 203 mm, plus 203.2 mm, and evaluated by straight lines between knots (bundle v3 `piecewise_linear`). | Smooth and monotone; uses both published numbers; the same reading as the convective cell's EF bounds. The table keeps Hazard's generic loader unchanged. Reading the band as 68% or 95% moves the house roof at 1.5 in between 33% and 41% (central 36%) (HRE-C020). | Step at the threshold; straight-line ramp between onset and full. | A source states the spread. |
| D-HRE-09 | Roof cost shares (of structure replacement cost new). House 3.75% = Hazus one-storey roofing 3% × repair factor 1.25. Office 2.7% = Hazus three-storey roof covering 1.1% + insulation 0.7%, × 1.25 × 1.20. Warehouse **12%**, judgment: 100,347 ft² × about $13.15/ft² ÷ $11.0M. | FEMA ratios for the house and office imply realistic 2025 prices: about $685 per square, and $16.20/ft² of roof (HRE-C008, C010). Hazus gives no warehouse breakdown. 12% sits between the $10 and $16/ft² rates implied for the office, and above the Hazus one-storey motel floor of 7.8% (HRE-C011, C022). JL chose a central curve with the range in SENSITIVITY (D-HRE-12); the 12% central value is proposed. | Warehouse 9% or 15%; office membrane only (1.65%); house without the repair factor (3.0%). | A published metal-roof rate (cost book). |
| D-HRE-10 | Walls and windows (Tier-4). House vinyl siding: 1.25–2.0 in, half on hail-facing elevations, $7/ft² × 1,602 ft². Windows: 1.5–2.5 in, a quarter of panes reached, $55/ft² residential and $80/ft² commercial, on the profile window areas (house 243 ft², warehouse 780 ft², office 7,207 ft²). Metal wall panels, overhead doors and masonry veneer are excluded. Hazard must pass `screening_assumption_set_id = HRE_T4_WALLS_WINDOWS_SCREENING_2026_10_18`. | No public hail threshold exists for siding or building glass (HRE-C006). Hail strikes walls obliquely and from one direction. The acknowledgement follows the wildfire × wind practice for Tier-4 numbers. | Withhold the unit (not zero). | Wall or window hail evidence. |
| D-HRE-11 | Range: 0–203.2 mm evaluates; above 203.2 mm withholds (`ABOVE_SOURCE_VALID_RANGE`); a negative diameter is rejected (`AXIS_OUTSIDE_VALID_RANGE`). Each curve is flat at its ceiling above the evidence (about 89 mm). | Hazard's physical cap is 203.2 mm; with raw MESH capped at 203.2 mm, Hazard's MESH75 reaches at most about 92 mm. Above the evidence the roof cover is fully replaced, which is the unit's own ceiling. Larger effects (deck punctures, interior leakage) are outside the unit. | Withhold above 100 mm. | Hazard changes its cap. |
