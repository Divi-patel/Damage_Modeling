# Decision log — convective_wind_real_estate model v0.1 / docs r1

Entries are **proposed** for JL's decision unless marked **JL decided** (2026-10-18). JL's decisions are scientific
choices for this proposal; the cell as a whole still needs owner review before any canonical use. Claims (CWRE-C…)
and sources (CWRE-S…) are in the [claim register](CLAIM_PARAMETER_REGISTER_convective_wind_real_estate__model_v0_1__docs_r1.csv)
and [source register](SOURCE_REGISTER_convective_wind_real_estate__model_v0_1__docs_r1.csv).

| D-CWRE-02 | Two governed pathways with the repository's existing IDs: `straight_line_convective` and `tornado_direct_hit`. Each has its own axis field (`convective_gust_3s_10m_mph`, `tornado_gust_3s_10m_mph`), its own records and its own evidence label. | D-CWRE-03 | **JL decided — convective-specific evidence: the EF scale degrees of damage**, not the hurricane building curves. Hurricane-curve reuse (as in the repository's station convective binding, CWRE-C019) is recorded as the documented alternative for the reviewer and is not built here. | The team practice is separate curves per hazard; the EF scale is the only public building-type damage evidence for convective wind (tornado surveys by building type). | D-CWRE-04 | Damage indicators: house = **FR12** (one- and two-family residences), warehouse = **MBS** (metal building systems), office = **LRB** (low-rise building, 1–4 storeys). | Profile matches: 1,800 ft² one-storey wood house; one-storey pre-engineered steel warehouse with eight overhead doors (MBS lists warehouses and large overhead doors); three-storey steel office with a TPO roof (LRB lists office buildings, single-ply roofs) (CWRE-C003 to C005). | WHB (tilt-up or heavy-timber warehouse); MRB (5–20 storeys) for the office. | Profile changes construction. |
| D-CWRE-05 | Axis = 3-second gust at 10 m in open terrain, mph, as Hazard delivers it, on both pathways. No terrain, height or averaging bridge in Damage. The EF speeds are 3-second gusts at the point of damage (height and exposure not stated); the difference is a flagged T4 bridge. | Hazard delivers ASCE 7-22 gusts at 10 m Exposure C and EF midpoint gusts (CWRE-C012); low-rise buildings sit near 10 m; the source states no exposure (CWRE-C002). | Convert EF speeds to Exposure C (no basis to do so). | A source fixes the EF reference height/exposure. |
| D-CWRE-06 | Curve form: expected cost over the ordered degrees of damage. Each degree's causing gust is lognormal with median = expected speed and β = ln(UB/LB)/(2·1.645) (bounds read as a 90% range); DR(v) = Σ (cᵢ − cᵢ₋₁)·Φ(ln(v/EXPᵢ)/βᵢ) with non-decreasing costs; tabulated every 1 mph from 0 to 255 mph and evaluated by straight lines between knots (bundle v3 `piecewise_linear`). | Uses all three published numbers per degree; smooth and monotone; the tabulation keeps Hazard's generic loader unchanged. Reading the bounds as 68% or 95% moves house damage at 120 mph by at most 5 points (CWRE-C011). | Step curve at the expected speeds (jumps of 40–50 points); ordered-state lognormal record (allows one β only). | A source states the bound percentile. |
| D-CWRE-07 | **JL decided — FEMA damage-state costs.** Slight 2%, Moderate 10%, Extensive 50%, Complete 100% (Hazus 7.0 Hurricane TM §8.1.4.3), with each EF degree assigned to the Hazus state its description matches (Tables 5-44, 5-74, 5-78). Proposed details: "threshold of visible damage" = 1% (half of Slight); house "broken glass" = 5% (one opening is Slight, more is Moderate; 5% is Hazus's in-between point); house "shifts off foundation" = Extensive (50%), with Complete as sensitivity. | No source prices EF degrees of damage (CWRE-C010); the Hazus ladder is FEMA's published building correspondence (expert judgment, CWRE-C006). The foundation-shift choice is the largest single cost sensitivity: house 32% → 48% at 120 mph straight-line. | Own cost ladder; claims-based costs (none by EF degree). | Repair-cost evidence by EF degree; JL changes the foundation-shift state. |
| D-CWRE-08 | Tornado records use the EF curve directly, **with no onset**. Tornado-specific effects (pressure drop, rotation, debris) are inside the surveyed EF damage and are not added separately. | The EF degrees are observed tornado damage at these gusts; NRI's tornado loss is already 4–9 times above ours, and the gap sits in Hazard's tornado path geometry, not the curve (CWRE-C015). | Apply the 96 mph onset to tornado too (would remove EF0 and most EF1 damage and widen the NRI gap). | Hazard revises tornado path dimensions; tornado claims by building type. |
| D-CWRE-09 | **JL decided — straight-line onset at a fixed 96 mph:** DR_SL(v) = max(0, (DR(v) − DR(96)) / (1 − DR(96))), so damage is zero at or below 96 mph and nearly unchanged at high gusts. 96 mph = 90% of the CONUS median ASCE 700-year gust (107 mph). | Without an onset, frequent 58–90 mph gusts drive straight-line yearly loss to 370–500 times NRI; with it, 13–35 times (CWRE-C014). An onset at 90% of each cell's own design gust gives the same national result (13–36 times) but needs a new Hazard input. Precedent: the solar straight-line curve is zero below 84–95% of the design gust (CWRE-C016). | Local onset (90% of each cell's design gust); 90 or 100 mph (sensitivity). | Hazard supplies a design gust per cell. |
| D-CWRE-10 | The straight-line records are labelled a **T4 transfer** of tornado-derived EF evidence. | The EF scale was developed from tornado damage and the report does not address straight-line wind; no source found establishes the EF speeds for downbursts (CWRE-C018). Wind loading on the envelope and frame depends on the gust in both cases, but tornado-only effects inside the EF observations may make the transfer conservative. | Withhold straight-line wind; hurricane-curve reuse for straight-line only. | A straight-line damage survey or loss study by building type. |
| D-CWRE-11 | **JL decided — no terrain adjustment.** The EF scale has no terrain classes; the open-terrain gust is used as delivered, and the remaining NRI gap is recorded rather than tuned. | No source basis for a terrain factor; tuning to NRI would be calibration against an incomplete county average. | Apply the hurricane cell's terrain ratios. | A convective terrain-effect source. |
| D-CWRE-12 | Denominator = structure replacement cost new, 100% on one failure unit, shared with `flood_real_estate` and `tropical_cyclone_wind_real_estate`; scenario dollars withheld until the consumer qualifies the budget. Contents is a separate failure unit, withheld (not zero). | One building, one value basis across perils; the Hazus ratios are of whole-building value. | Market, assessed or depreciated value; contents folded in. | Consumer value qualification. |
| D-CWRE-13 | Out of scope: tropical-cyclone wind (→ `tropical_cyclone_wind_real_estate`), hail (future hail × real estate cell), lightning, rainfall flooding, loss of use and business interruption. A tornado spawned by a tropical cyclone is priced in one cell only (consumer partition); all perils on one building in one event together stay at or below 100% of structure value (consumer). | Matches Hazard's sub-peril routing and the sibling real-estate cells. | — | Hazard adds event identity across perils. |
| D-CWRE-14 | Range: 0–255 mph evaluates; above 255 mph withholds (`ABOVE_SOURCE_VALID_RANGE`); a negative gust is rejected (`AXIS_OUTSIDE_VALID_RANGE`, the Hazard loader's code). | Covers Hazard's physical bound (252.8 mph) and the EF5 band top (253 mph); the curves reach their ceiling well inside the range. | Clamp above the range. | Hazard changes its bound. |
| D-CWRE-15 | An EF rating is never evaluated as a speed. Hazard's EF band midpoint gust (75, 98, 123, 150.5, 183, 227 mph) is accepted as the tornado gust and flagged as a damage-inferred T4 bridge. | Same treatment as the repository's station convective binding (CWRE-C019); the wind-farm v2 proposal also refuses EF ratings as speeds. | Map EF rating to damage directly. | Hazard delivers a local tornado wind. |
